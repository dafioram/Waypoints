Pokémon main gamesHow close each remake, extended version and sequel is to the original

22 games and pairs across 9 regions, from Red and Blue in 1998 to Legends: Z-A in 2025, grouped by region so you can see what grew out of what. Each has its critic score, how long it takes, and what differs between the two versions of a pair.

English releases only. The spin-offs, such as Mystery Dungeon and Pokémon Snap, are on their own page.

How the labels work

The labels are my own grouping, based on how Wikipedia, Bulbapedia and the reviews I read describe each game. They are not Nintendo’s terms.

  • Original. The first game of its region, and the one everything else in the row is measured against.
  • Same game. A port or re-release of a game that already exists. Small fixes at most.
  • Extended version. The same region and core story with new content added, often called a third version. Yellow, Crystal, Emerald, Platinum and Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon.
  • Remake. Rebuilt for newer hardware with the same map and story, plus features from later games.
  • Reworked remake. The same region and story, but a changed way of playing. Only Let’s Go is in this group.
  • New game, same world. A different story set in the same region or time: a sequel or a prequel, not a version of the original.

Re-releases such as the 3DS Virtual Console versions are the “Same game” label and appear inside the region’s page, not in the tree. Paid add-ons are marked “Add-on”.

Quick answers

How close is each remake to its original?
FireRed and LeafGreen, HeartGold and SoulSilver, Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire, and Brilliant Diamond and Shining Pearl all keep the map, the story and the gym order. What differs is the extras: HeartGold and SoulSilver add a following Pokémon and the Battle Frontier, Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire add a new episode and Mega Evolution, and the Switch remake leaves out the extended version’s additions. Let’s Go is the odd one. It keeps Kanto’s story and changes how you catch and battle.See Kanto, Johto, Hoenn, Sinnoh.
Which games look like remakes but are not?
Black 2 and White 2 are direct sequels set in Unova. Legends: Arceus is set in Sinnoh centuries before Diamond and Pearl and plays as an action RPG. Legends: Z-A returns to Kalos, to Lumiose City only, with real-time battles. All three are new games in the same world.See Unova, Sinnoh, Kalos.
Is an extended version worth playing if I know the original?
Critics were lukewarm about most of them. Electronic Gaming Monthly said Crystal’s additions do not change the game at all, Eurogamer recommended Platinum to fans but not to traditional RPG players, and Polygon said Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon change mostly at the end. IGN was warmer about Emerald, naming it the best starting point for newcomers to Ruby and Sapphire.See Kanto, Johto, Hoenn, Sinnoh, Alola.
Can I still play the originals?
Red, Blue and Yellow, and Gold, Silver and Crystal, were sold on the 3DS Virtual Console. That store closed to new purchases on Mar 27, 2023, and TheGamer says earlier purchases can still be redownloaded. FireRed and LeafGreen came to Switch and Switch 2 on Feb 27, 2026, as the 2004 games. I found no re-release of Ruby, Sapphire or Emerald, or of the Nintendo DS and 3DS originals.See Kanto, Johto, Hoenn.
Which main games scored best and worst?
Among those with a Metacritic score, the highest are Black and White, HeartGold and SoulSilver, Sun and Moon (all 87) and X and Y (87 and 88). The older games have only GameRankings scores, where Silver reached 91%. The lowest are Scarlet and Violet (72 and 71) and Legends: Z-A (70 on Switch).See Paldea, Kalos, Unova, Johto.
Do the paired versions really differ?
Always in which Pokémon you can catch, and in the version legendary. From Black and White on, more changes: the Black City and White Forest, the Opelucid gym leader, Sword and Shield’s gym leaders and rivals, and Scarlet and Violet’s professor and school. Each game’s box below has a short line on what its pair differs by.See Unova, Galar, Paldea.

1Generation

Kanto

Red, Blue and Yellow

Red and Blue are the first pair. Feb 27, 1996 in Japan. Sep 28, 1998 in North America.

  • 4 games or pairs
  • 79–81 on Metacritic

You leave Pallet Town, win eight badges, beat the Elite Four and try to fill a Pokédex of 151. Every later game is a variation on this: catch, battle in turns, and trade between two versions to finish the set. It has the most re-releases of any line, and the most different ones.

The games, and how close each is to the original

  1. Original

    Red and Blue

    Game Boy. Sep 28, 1998 in North America. Oct 5, 1999 in Europe.

    The original. The English games are built on Japanese Blue, which upgraded the graphics and removed glitches. Japan’s Green was never released in English.

    How the pair differs. Eleven Pokémon each. Red has Ekans, Oddish, Mankey, Growlithe, Scyther and Electabuzz. Blue has Sandshrew, Vulpix, Meowth, Bellsprout, Magmar and Pinsir. The box mascots are Charizard and Blastoise.

    IGN gave Red 10/10 and called the challenge of catching them all its biggest draw. GameSpot gave Blue 8.8.

  2. Extended version

    Yellow

    Game Boy. Oct 18, 1999 in North America. Jun 16, 2000 in Europe.

    Same map, same 151 and the same story. Pikachu is your only starter and follows you, your rival starts with Eevee, Jessie and James appear, and the look is closer to the anime. There is a Pikachu’s Beach mini-game and Game Boy Printer support for Pokédex stickers.

    GameRankings
    Yellow 85%
    Story

    With extras

    GameSpot called it a stopgap. RPGFan called the additions insulting, and the Daily Telegraph called it a marketing exercise. It sold a million copies in ten days in North America.

  3. Same game

    Red, Blue and Yellow on 3DS

    Nintendo 3DS Virtual Console. Feb 2016.

    The same three games, with simulated link-cable trading and battling added. The 3DS eShop closed to new purchases on Mar 27, 2023, and TheGamer says earlier purchases can still be redownloaded.

  4. Remake

    FireRed and LeafGreen

    Game Boy Advance. Sep 9, 2004 in North America. Oct 1, 2004 in Europe.

    The same story and the same Kanto, redrawn for the Game Boy Advance. It brings in later features, such as abilities, the Steel and Dark types, friendship, weather and breeding, and adds the Sevii Islands, which hold Johto Pokémon.

    How the pair differs. FireRed has Ekans, Growlithe, Scyther, Psyduck, Skarmory and Deoxys in its Attack Forme. LeafGreen has Sandshrew, Vulpix, Slowpoke, Staryu, Pinsir, Azurill and Deoxys in its Defense Forme. Little else differs.

    Metacritic
    81
    Story
    25–30 hours
    With extras
    45–55 hours
    Everything
    115–155 hours

    Wikipedia’s Red, Blue and Yellow page gives a Metacritic score of 81 for the pair. I found no individual review quotes for them.

  5. Same game

    FireRed and LeafGreen on Switch

    Nintendo Switch and Switch 2. Feb 27, 2026.

    The 2004 games as they were, sold one at a time for $19.99, not as part of Nintendo Switch Online. They support Pokémon HOME and local wireless battling and have no online play. Video Games Chronicle says most e-Reader features are in the Japanese original only.

  6. Reworked remake

    Let’s Go, Pikachu! and Let’s Go, Eevee!

    Nintendo Switch. Nov 16, 2018.

    Kanto again, with Yellow’s story. The big change is how you play: wild battles are replaced by catching in the style of Pokémon GO, your partner Pikachu or Eevee goes everywhere with you, the five HMs become Secret Techniques, and a second player can join for local co-op. Only the original 151 appear, plus Meltan, Melmetal and a few Alolan forms.

    How the pair differs. Pikachu has Grimer and Sandshrew. Eevee has Meowth and Vulpix. Your partner cannot evolve.

    Metacritic
    Pikachu 79, Eevee 80
    Story
    20–25 hours
    With extras
    35–45 hours
    Everything
    85–100 hours

    HowLongToBeat, via Deku Deals, says 25, 36 and 54½.

    IGN gave 8.3 and GameSpot 8, with praise for the catching system and the Kanto redesign. One review noted missing Pokémon and awkward Joy-Con catching. Players review-bombed it, giving a user score near 53 at launch.

What changes across this row

  • Yellow is the only extended version, and it changes the starter and the tone, not the map.
  • FireRed and LeafGreen keep the story and add later features such as abilities and the Steel and Dark types. They are the closest copy of the story.
  • Let’s Go keeps the story and replaces the battle-and-catch loop, so it plays unlike the other three.
  • Green, the Japanese original, is not in the English line-up.

What critics said

The originals scored 88% on GameRankings (Red and Blue) and Yellow 85%. Yellow took the harshest reviews for small additions. FireRed and LeafGreen sit at 81 on Metacritic. Let’s Go was praised as a fresh way into Kanto and a bridge from Pokémon GO, but some found it thinner than fans hoped.

Short answer: The 2004 remake is the same game in new clothes. Yellow is Red and Blue with Pikachu. Let’s Go is the same trip with different rules.

2Generation

Johto

Gold, Silver and Crystal

Gold and Silver are the first pair. Nov 21, 1999 in Japan. Oct 15, 2000 in North America.

  • 3 games or pairs
  • 87 on Metacritic

Three years after Kanto, you cross into Johto and later return to Kanto itself. This is where the clock, held items, breeding, shiny Pokémon and two new types arrived.

The games, and how close each is to the original

  1. Original

    Gold and Silver

    Game Boy Color. Oct 15, 2000 in North America. Apr 6, 2001 in Europe.

    The original. It adds a real-time clock, held items, the Pokégear, breeding, Steel and Dark types, the Special stat split in two, shiny Pokémon (about 1 in 8192) and 100 new species. It can trade with Generation 1, using a Move Deleter to remove moves the old games lack.

    How the pair differs. Gold has Mankey, Growlithe, Spinarak, Gligar, Teddiursa and Mantine. Silver has Vulpix, Meowth, Ledyba, Delibird, Skarmory and Phanpy. Gold’s Rainbow Wing calls Ho-Oh and Silver’s Silver Wing calls Lugia.

    IGN gave Gold 10/10 and called it masterful. GameSpot gave it 8.8. Reviewers praised the added length and the clock.

  2. Extended version

    Crystal

    Game Boy Color. Jul 30, 2001 in North America. Nov 2, 2001 in Europe.

    Same Johto and same story. It adds animated Pokémon in battle, a female player (Kris), the Battle Tower, and a bigger role for Suicune with a new character, Eusine. Wikipedia calls it an enhanced edition of Gold and Silver, not a third version. Its Japan-only online features did not reach English.

    GameRankings

    GameSpot gave 8.4 and IGN 9. Electronic Gaming Monthly said the additions do not alter the game-playing experience at all. It is the lowest-selling main game, at about 6.3 million.

  3. Same game

    Gold, Silver and Crystal on 3DS

    Nintendo 3DS Virtual Console. Gold and Silver Sep 22, 2017. Crystal Jan 28, 2018.

    The same games. Wikipedia’s page describes no changes for either release. The store that sold them closed to new purchases in 2023.

  4. Remake

    HeartGold and SoulSilver

    Nintendo DS. Mar 14, 2010 in North America. Mar 26, 2010 in Europe.

    A remake of Gold and Silver that takes features from Crystal. The Johto-then-Kanto structure is kept, down to Red waiting on Mt. Silver. The lead Pokémon follows you, a Pokéwalker pedometer comes in the box, and it adds the Pokéathlon, Pal Park, the Battle Frontier and a new Safari Zone. Bulbapedia lists the physical and special move split too.

    How the pair differs. Ho-Oh is the legendary in HeartGold and Lugia in SoulSilver, and the two appear on the boxes. Wikipedia names no other difference.

    Metacritic

    With extras

    Famitsu gave 37/40, and 1UP wrote that they are easily the best Pokémon game yet. GameSpot scored it lower for a lack of innovation. They won the 2010 Golden Joystick Award for Portable Game of the Year.

What changes across this row

  • Crystal changes the presentation and adds a tower and a new story thread, but the world is the same.
  • HeartGold and SoulSilver keep the whole two-region shape and add the Generation 4 rules plus a lot around the edges, such as a following Pokémon and the Pokéwalker.
  • The English Crystal has no online features, unlike the Japanese one.

What critics said

Gold and Silver scored 89% and 91% on GameRankings. Crystal scored 80%, and reviewers called its changes small. HeartGold and SoulSilver reached 87 on Metacritic, the highest of the remakes.

Short answer: Crystal is Gold and Silver with extras. HeartGold and SoulSilver are the same adventure with a lot added, and the best-reviewed of the Johto games on Metacritic.

3Generation

Hoenn

Ruby, Sapphire and Emerald

Ruby and Sapphire are the first pair. 2002 in Japan. Mar 19, 2003 in North America.

  • 3 games or pairs
  • 76–83 on Metacritic

In each version a villain team, Magma or Aqua, wakes an ancient Pokémon. This is where double battles, Abilities and Natures began. These games cannot trade with the first two generations.

The games, and how close each is to the original

  1. Original

    Ruby and Sapphire

    Game Boy Advance. Mar 19, 2003 in North America. Jul 25, 2003 in Europe.

    The original. It adds double and multi battles, Abilities, Natures, Contests, secret bases, a four-player link and about 135 new Pokémon. Time still passes but there is no day and night split.

    How the pair differs. Ruby has Team Magma and Groudon, and Sapphire has Team Aqua and Kyogre. Ruby has Seedot, Mawile, Zangoose, Solrock and Latios. Sapphire has Lotad, Sableye, Seviper, Lunatone and Latias.

    Emerald

    Game Boy Advance. May 1, 2005 in North America. Oct 21, 2005 in Europe.

    Same Hoenn and story, with both Magma and Aqua in one game and Rayquaza called to settle Groudon and Kyogre. It adds the Battle Frontier, animated battle sprites, gym rematches and a stronger Steven. English copies dropped the e-Reader support.

    Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire

    Nintendo 3DS. Nov 21, 2014 in North America. Nov 28, 2014 in Europe.

    A remake of Ruby and Sapphire with some Emerald features. The region, story and eight gyms are kept. It brings in Mega Evolution, Pokémon-Amie, Super Training, the physical and special split and unlimited TMs, and adds Primal Reversion for Groudon and Kyogre, flying with Latios or Latias, 20 new Mega Evolutions and a new episode with Rayquaza and Deoxys called the Delta Episode.

    How the pair differs. Omega Ruby has Team Magma and Primal Groudon. Alpha Sapphire has Team Aqua and Primal Kyogre.

What changes across this row

  • Emerald merges the two story versions, so it is the only Hoenn game with both villain teams.
  • The remake keeps the region, the story and the eight gyms, and adds systems from Generations 4 to 6.

What critics said

Ruby and Sapphire scored 82 on Metacritic and Emerald 76. Reviewers liked the depth of Hoenn but said little changed from Johto. Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire scored 83 and 82, with praise for the 3D Hoenn and the online features.

Short answer: IGN named Emerald the best starting point for a newcomer to the original pair. The remake keeps the story and the map and adds new systems around them.

4Generation

Sinnoh

Diamond, Pearl and Platinum

Diamond and Pearl are the first pair. 2006 in Japan. Apr 22, 2007 in North America.

  • 4 games or pairs
  • 73–85 on Metacritic

A region built around Mt. Coronet, where the version legendaries wait at the summit. These games brought online trading and battling and the physical and special move split. The line has three follow-ups.

  1. Original

    Diamond and Pearl

    Nintendo DS. Apr 22, 2007 in North America. Jul 27, 2007 in Europe.

    The original. It adds Wi-Fi trading and battling with voice chat, the physical and special split, the Pokétch watch on the touch screen, the Underground, five times of day, 107 new Pokémon and transfer from Game Boy Advance cartridges through Pal Park.

    How the pair differs. Diamond has Dialga, Seel, Scyther, Murkrow, Larvitar, Aron and Stunky. Pearl has Palkia, Slowpoke, Pinsir, Misdreavus, Houndour, Bagon and Glameow.

    Same Sinnoh and the same Team Galactic, with the characters Charon and Looker added, a new area called the Distortion World, a new Giratina form, the Battle Frontier, a Wi-Fi Plaza and an expanded Pokédex that lets you meet evolved Pokémon sooner.     Legends: Arceus

    Nintendo Switch. Jan 28, 2022.

    Not a remake. It is set in Hisui, which is Sinnoh centuries before Diamond and Pearl, and is an action RPG: you catch Pokémon in the open without battling, wild Pokémon can attack you, and there are Alpha and Noble Pokémon. Nintendo’s own accounts called it a pre-remake of the Sinnoh games.

What changes across this row

  • Platinum changes the story’s focus to Giratina and adds a new area, but it is Sinnoh as before.
  • The remake leaves out Platinum’s changes, so it matches Diamond and Pearl more than Platinum.
  • Legends: Arceus shares a map shape and a world with Sinnoh, but it is a different kind of game.

What critics said

Diamond and Pearl scored 85 on Metacritic and Platinum 84. The remake’s 73 is the lowest of any remake, and its chibi look divided reviewers. Legends: Arceus scored 83 and was praised for changing the series’ formula, though some critics disliked the graphics.

Short answer: Platinum is the fuller version of Diamond and Pearl. The remake is faithful but leaves out Platinum’s additions. Arceus is its own game.

Unova

Black and White

Black and White are the first pair. 2010 in Japan. Mar 6, 2011 in North America.

  • 2 games or pairs

A region modelled on New York City, where the games begin with only new Pokémon. Its follow-up is a pair of real sequels, the first in the series, rather than a third version.

Opelucid City looks futuristic in Black and traditional in White, and its gym leader is Drayden in Black and Iris in White. Black has Weedle, Murkrow, Houndour and Gothita. White has Caterpie, Misdreavus, Poochyena and Solosis.

    Famitsu gave a perfect 40/40 and IGN gave 9. Reviewers were split on some designs and some felt it did not innovate enough.

  2. New game, same world

    Black 2 and White 2

    Nintendo DS. Oct 7, 2012 in North America. Oct 12, 2012 in Europe.

    Direct sequels, the first in the series, not remakes. They start in Aspertia City in the west of Unova instead of Nuvema Town, with a new Team Plasma led by Colress. They add the Pokémon World Tournament, Hidden Grottoes, PokéStar Studios, a Key System for difficulty and an expanded Pokédex.

    How the pair differs. Black Kyurem is in Black 2 and White Kyurem in White 2. N’s companion is Zekrom in Black 2 and Reshiram in White 2. Black 2 has the Black Tower and Black City, and White 2 has the White Treehollow and White Forest.

    Metacritic
    80

    With extras
    65–80 hours
    Everything
    185–215 hours

    IGN gave Black 2 9.6 and Nintendo Life called it the best in the series to date. GameSpot gave Black 2 6.5 and Game Informer 7.5 for leaning on the originals.

What changes across this row

  • Black 2 and White 2 are set in Unova with new cities and a new story, so they are sequels in the same world.
  • This is the one line with no remake and no third version.

What critics said

Black and White scored 87 on Metacritic and Black 2 and White 2 scored 80. Reviewers liked the sequels’ new features and said they leaned on the first pair.

Short answer: Black 2 and White 2 are a new story in the same place, not a rework of Black and White.

Kalos

X and Y

X and Y are the first pair. Oct 12, 2013 worldwide.

  • 2 games or pairs
  • 70–88 on Metacritic

The first main games in 3D and the first released everywhere on one day. The region is modelled on France. Its only follow-up came twelve years later and stays in one city.

The games, and how close each is to the original

  1. Original

    X and Y

    Nintendo 3DS. Oct 12, 2013 worldwide.

    The original. It adds the Fairy type, Mega Evolution, 72 new species, character customisation, Super Training, Pokémon-Amie, Wonder Trade and horde encounters.

    How the pair differs. Xerneas is the legendary in X and Yveltal in Y, and each version has its own exclusive Pokémon. Wikipedia names none of them.

    Metacritic
    X 87, Y 88
    Story
    30–35 hours
    With extras
    50–60 hours
    Everything
    130–145 hours

    Polygon gave 9.5 and IGN 9. The story and the linear structure drew criticism. It sold 4 million in its first weekend, the fastest for a 3DS game then.

  2. New game, same world

    Legends: Z-A

    Nintendo Switch and Switch 2. Oct 16, 2025.

    A sequel set five years after X and Y, in Lumiose City only. Battles are in real time instead of turns, Wild Zones hold Pokémon among the buildings, and Mega Evolution returns with new Mega forms. Switch 2 has an enhanced edition.

    Metacritic
    Switch 70, Switch 2 78
    Story
    25–30 hours
    With extras
    30–40 hours
    Everything
    60–75 hours

    HowLongToBeat, via Deku Deals, says 28, 40½ and 66½.

    Critics praised the battle system and the Switch 2 performance. They called the city setting restrictive. Nintendo Life described the Switch visuals as lifeless and flat.

  3. Add-on

    Mega Dimension

    Add-on for Z-A. Dec 10, 2025.

    A paid expansion centred on Hoopa, with two Mega forms for Raichu and other new Megas.

What changes across this row

  • Legends: Z-A is a different kind of game, with real-time battles, but it takes place in a part of X and Y’s map.
  • No remake or extended version exists for X and Y.

What critics said

X and Y scored 87 and 88 on Metacritic. Legends: Z-A scored 70 on Switch and 78 on Switch 2, and critics praised the Switch 2 performance.

Short answer: Z-A is a spin on X and Y’s world with different rules. It is not a version of them.

Alola

Sun, Moon, Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon

Sun and Moon are the first pair. Nov 18, 2016 worldwide.

  • 84–87 on Metacritic

A Hawaii-inspired archipelago with no gym challenge. You do island trials and meet kahunas instead. The sequel pair is an extended version of the same adventure.

The games, and how close each is to the original

  1. Original

    Sun and Moon

    Nintendo 3DS. Nov 18, 2016 worldwide (Europe Nov 23).

    The original. It adds Z-Moves, Alolan forms of older Pokémon, Island Trials in place of gyms, the Rotom Pokédex and Festival Plaza, and removes the HMs in favour of summoned helper Pokémon.

    How the pair differs. Solgaleo is in Sun and Lunala in Moon. Moon’s clock runs 12 hours ahead of Sun’s. Each has its own flute and altar. Sun has Buzzwole, Kartana and Alolan Vulpix. Moon has Pheromosa, Celesteela and Alolan Sandshrew.

    Metacritic
    87
    Story
    35–40 hours
    With extras
    60–70 hours
    Everything
    145–165 hours

    Nintendo Life gave 10/10 and IGN 9. Eurogamer called it the best generation in more than a decade. Polygon criticised the linearity.

  2. Extended version

    Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon

    Nintendo 3DS. Nov 17, 2017 worldwide.

    Enhanced versions with the same mechanics and graphics. The story is changed, with Necrozma replacing Lusamine as the main antagonist, Ultra Wormholes, four new Ultra Beasts, the Ultra Recon Squad and Team Rainbow Rocket in the post-game.

    How the pair differs. In Ultra Sun, Nebby becomes Solgaleo and the Ultra Beast to catch is Blacephalon. In Ultra Moon, Nebby becomes Lunala and the Ultra Beast is Stakataka. The Ultra Recon Squad has different members in each.

    Metacritic
    84

    With extras
    65–75 hours
    Everything
    155–175 hours

    Critics liked the added features. Some said the story was too close to Sun and Moon for most of its length, and Polygon noted the changes come near the end.

What changes across this row

  • Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon keep the island, characters and look, and change the second half of the story.
  • They are the last main games on 3DS.

What critics said

Sun and Moon scored 87 and the Ultra pair 84 on Metacritic. Reviewers praised Sun and Moon heavily and liked the Ultra games’ extras, but said the story was close to the first pair for most of its length.

Short answer: Ultra Sun and Ultra Moon change the second half of the story and add content. If you have played Sun or Moon, most of it will feel familiar.

A region inspired by the United Kingdom. A large open Wild Area sits in the middle, and not every older Pokémon made it in.

The games, and how close each is to the original

    Sword and Shield

    How the pair differs. Zacian is in Sword and Zamazenta in Shield. Sword has the gym leaders Bea and Gordie and Shield has Allister and Melony. Sword has Galarian Farfetch’d and Gothita. Shield has Galarian Ponyta and Larvitar.

    45–55 hours

Sword and Shield scored 80 on Metacritic. Praise went to the exploration and the raid battles. Criticism went to the missing Pokémon and the lack of depth.

Paldea

Scarlet and Violet

Scarlet and Violet are the first pair. Nov 18, 2022.

  • 1 game or pair
  • 71–72 on Metacritic

An open world inspired by the Iberian Peninsula, with three stories you can do in any order. It is the second best-selling line, behind Red and Blue, and the lowest-rated main line on Metacritic.

The games, and how close each is to the original

  1. Original

    Scarlet and Violet

    Nintendo Switch. Nov 18, 2022.

    The original. It has a connected open world, Auto Battle, three storylines (Victory Road, Path of Legends and Starfall Street), Terastallization and online co-op for up to four players, including Tera Raids.

    How the pair differs. Koraidon is in Scarlet and Miraidon in Violet. Professor Sada runs the lab in Scarlet and Professor Turo in Violet. The school is Naranja Academy in Scarlet and Uva Academy in Violet. Scarlet has the ancient-past Paradox Pokémon, such as Great Tusk, and Violet the future ones, such as Iron Treads.

    Metacritic
    Scarlet 72, Violet 71
    Story
    30–35 hours
    With extras
    55–65 hours
    Everything
    110–135 hours

    Eurogamer called them comprehensive technical failures against other Switch games, and IGN gave 6. GameSpot praised the non-linearity. Patches fixed many bugs.

  2. Add-on

    The Teal Mask

    Paid add-on. Sep 13, 2023.

    The first part of The Hidden Treasure of Area Zero, focused on Ogerpon.

  3. Add-on

    The Indigo Disk

    Paid add-on. Dec 14, 2023.

    The second part, focused on Terapagos, followed by the Mochi Mayhem epilogue on Jan 11, 2024.

What changes across this row

  • No remake, extended version or sequel exists yet. Two paid add-ons and an epilogue extended it.

What critics said

Scarlet and Violet scored 72 and 71 on Metacritic, the lowest for a main game. They sold over 10 million in three days. Reviewers liked the freedom and disliked the glitches and low frame rate.

Short answer: One game, two add-ons. Wikipedia says patches in 2022 and 2023 fixed bugs while performance problems persisted.
