Pokémon spin-offsHow each series’ games relate, from Mystery Dungeon to Pokopia

22 series and 39 games outside the main series, from 1999 to 2026. Each series has one entry: its games in order, how they relate, critic scores, play times where I found them, and what differs between paired versions.

English releases only. Picross and mobile or free-to-play games are left out. The main series is on its own page.

How the labels work

The labels are my own grouping, based on how Wikipedia and the reviews I read describe each game. They are not Nintendo’s terms, and the colours match the main-series page, though a few labels differ.

  • First game. The first game in the series. In a series with one game, that game.
  • Same game. A port or re-release of a game that already exists.
  • Extended version. The same game with more added, such as a third version or an enlarged re-release. Explorers of Sky and Pokkén Tournament DX.
  • Remake. Rebuilt for newer hardware with the same core game. Only Rescue Team DX is in this group.
  • Companion game. A different game on another system, released alongside. Only Puzzle Challenge is in this group.
  • Follow-up game. A new game in the same series, with its own story or features. Most are sequels.

Re-releases such as Virtual Console and Switch Online versions are the “Same game” label and appear inside each series’ entry, not on the timeline.

Quick answers

Which spin-offs score best and worst?
On Metacritic, Pokopia leads with 89, then Puzzle League (81), Conquest (80), and Pokkén DX and New Pokémon Snap (79). The lowest are Pokémon Dash (46), My Pokémon Ranch (47) and Battle Revolution (53). A few games have only GameRankings scores: Pokémon Stadium (78.60%), Pinball (81.73%), the Trading Card Game (81.25%) and Typing Adventure (69.22%).See Pokopia, Puzzle League, Conquest, Dash.
Which can I play on Switch now?
Five are Switch games: New Pokémon Snap, Detective Pikachu Returns, Pokkén Tournament DX and Rescue Team DX on Switch, and Pokopia on Switch 2. Nintendo Switch Online, with a membership, adds Pokémon Snap, Stadium and Stadium 2, Red Rescue Team, the Trading Card Game and XD.See Snap, Detective Pikachu, Pokkén Tournament, Mystery Dungeon, Pokopia, Stadium, Trading Card Game, Colosseum and XD.
Do any of these need a main-series game?
Several do. Battle Revolution needs Diamond or Pearl for most of what it offers, and My Pokémon Ranch takes Pokémon from Diamond and Pearl. The Stadium games read Game Boy cartridges through the Transfer Pak, and Colosseum trades with the Game Boy Advance games.See Battle Revolution, My Pokémon Ranch, Stadium, Colosseum and XD.
Where should I start with Mystery Dungeon?
Rescue Team DX remakes the first game and is on Switch. Nintendo World Report calls Super Mystery Dungeon, on 3DS, the easiest entry point, though it also calls its story the weakest in the series. IGN said Gates to Infinity fails as both a Pokémon game and a Mystery Dungeon game.See Mystery Dungeon.
Which are remakes or enlarged versions?
Rescue Team DX remakes the first Mystery Dungeon games. Explorers of Sky is a third version of Explorers, and Pokkén DX is an enlarged version of the Wii U game. Re-releases, such as Virtual Console and Switch Online versions, are small rows inside each entry.See Mystery Dungeon, Pokkén Tournament.

2000First in English

Stadium

Pokémon Stadium and Stadium 2

Battle and fighting

  • 2 games
  • 78 on Metacritic

Turn-based 3D battles on the Nintendo 64. You bring your teams over from the Game Boy games with the Transfer Pak, then fight in cups, a gym leader castle and a set of mini-games.

The games, and how they relate

  1. First game

    Pokémon Stadium

    Nintendo 64. Mar 6, 2000 in North America. Apr 7, 2000 in Europe.

    All 151 Pokémon in turn-based 3D battles. It has four Cups with 80 battles in all, the Gym Leader Castle, a Mewtwo battle, the Pokémon Lab and a Kids Club with nine mini-games. The Transfer Pak moves Pokémon in from Red, Blue and Yellow, and lets you play the Game Boy game on the TV.

    GameRankings
    78.6%

    GameRankings averages 26 reviews at 78.60%.

  2. Follow-up game

    Pokémon Stadium 2

    Nintendo 64. Mar 26, 2001 in North America. Oct 10, 2001 in Europe.

    Adds the Gold, Silver and Crystal Pokémon, so all 251 are playable. The Gym Leader Castle returns, a Game Boy Tower is new, and the Transfer Pak reads both generations of Game Boy games. A Mini-Game Park has 12 mini-games and quizzes.

    Metacritic
    78
    GameRankings
    73.31%

    Critics said it plays much like the first game, with a weaker soundtrack and announcer, and that it repeats itself. GameRankings averages 18 reviews at 73.31%.

  3. Same game

    Stadium and Stadium 2 on Nintendo Switch Online

    Nintendo Switch Online, Nintendo Classics (N64). 2023. Stadium on Apr 12, 2023.

    Both games, without the Transfer Pak features. Stadium has online play.

What changes across this series

  • Stadium 2 raises the roster from 151 to 251 and adds the Game Boy Tower.
  • Both use the Transfer Pak. Stadium reads Red, Blue and Yellow, and Stadium 2 reads those plus Gold, Silver and Crystal.
  • Japan’s first Stadium, with 40 Pokémon, was never released in English.

What critics said

Stadium 2 holds a Metacritic score of 78. Its GameRankings score, 73.31%, is lower than the first game’s 78.60%. Critics said the second game feels like the first.

Short answer: Same kind of game, with a bigger roster in the second. Both are on Switch Online without the Transfer Pak.

2004First in English

Colosseum and XD

Pokémon Colosseum and Pokémon XD: Gale of Darkness

Battle and fighting

  • 2 games
  • 64 to 73 on Metacritic

Role-playing games on the GameCube, set in Orre, where you snag Shadow Pokémon from other trainers. A keengamer retrospective notes that Colosseum has no gyms and no wild Pokémon, and that all of its battles are doubles.

The games, and how they relate

  1. First game

    Pokémon Colosseum

    GameCube. Mar 22, 2004 in North America. May 14, 2004 in Europe.

    You play Wes, a former member of Team Snagem, and snag Shadow Pokémon from the Cipher gang. Modes include Quick Battle, Colosseum battles that pay Poké Coupons, and four-player Gang Battle. It trades with Ruby, Sapphire, FireRed, LeafGreen and Emerald. North American pre-orders got a bonus disc with Jirachi.

    Metacritic
    73
    GameRankings
    73.46%
  2. Follow-up game

    Pokémon XD: Gale of Darkness

    GameCube. Oct 3, 2005 in North America. Nov 18, 2005 in Europe.

    Set in Orre five years later, with a new hero, Michael, who starts with an Eevee. Shadow Lugia is on the cover. Wild Pokémon return as Poké Spots, where you lure them with bait, and Battle Bingo is a new mode in the Realgam Tower. Munchlax and Bonsly debut here.

    Metacritic
    64
    GameRankings
    66.91%

    Reviews were mixed, at 6 to 7 out of 10, and noted reused Colosseum areas. A keengamer retrospective says XD improves nearly everything.

  3. Same game

    XD on Nintendo Switch Online

    Nintendo Switch Online, GameCube. March 2026.

    XD joined Nintendo Switch Online’s GameCube selection in March 2026, according to Game Informer’s recap of the Feb 27, 2026 Pokémon Presents.

What changes across this series

  • XD is set five years after Colosseum, with a new hero.
  • Colosseum has no wild Pokémon. XD adds them back as Poké Spots.
  • Shadow Pokémon, the ones you snag, are in both.

What critics said

Colosseum scored 73 on Metacritic and XD 64, so the sequel scored lower even though a retrospective says it improves on the first game.

Short answer: Colosseum is where Shadow Pokémon start. XD brings wild Pokémon back and is the one on Switch Online.

2006First in English

Mystery Dungeon

Six games from Rescue Team to Rescue Team DX

Dungeons and strategy

  • 6 games or pairs
  • 54 to 69 on Metacritic

Roguelike dungeon crawlers from Chunsoft, now Spike Chunsoft. You are a human turned into a Pokémon, you explore randomly generated floors one turn at a time, and you recruit Pokémon as you go.

The games, and how they relate

  1. First game

    Red Rescue Team and Blue Rescue Team

    Game Boy Advance (Red) and Nintendo DS (Blue). Sep 18, 2006 in North America. Nov 10, 2006 in Europe.

    The first. A personality quiz picks your Pokémon from 16, and you choose a partner from the rest, never one of your own type.

    How the pair differs. Red is on Game Boy Advance and Blue on DS. They are mostly the same, but Blue uses both DS screens and has better graphics and sound. Each has six exclusive Pokémon, the starters.

    Metacritic
    Red 67, Blue 62

    1UP gave A-, IGN 6.5, GameSpot 5.2 and Game Informer 3. GameSpot said you should probably skip it. It was nominated for Children’s Game of the Year at the 10th DICE Awards.

  2. Same game

    Red Rescue Team on Nintendo Switch Online

    Nintendo Switch Online, Game Boy Advance. Aug 9, 2024.

    The Game Boy Advance game joined Nintendo Classics, per Wikipedia.

  3. Follow-up game

    Explorers of Time and Explorers of Darkness

    Nintendo DS. Apr 20, 2008 in North America. Jul 4, 2008 in Europe.

    A sequel to Rescue Team. It brings in Generation 4 Pokémon, better Wi-Fi and touch controls, and lets you send a teammate to a friend, so a team can be larger than four. The story follows Grovyle, the Time Gears and Dusknoir.

    How the pair differs. Time has Celebi, Combee, Lucario, Pachirisu and Riolu. Darkness has Burmy, Buneary, Lopunny, Mewtwo and Rotom. Each can get the other’s Pokémon through Wonder Mail.

    Metacritic
    Time 60, Darkness 59
    GameRankings
    Time 62.39%, Darkness 61.25%

    GameSpot and IGN gave 6.5 out of 10 and GamesRadar+ gave 2.5 out of 5. Critics praised the story, soundtrack and Wi-Fi, and criticised the graphics and the repetition.

  4. Extended version

    Explorers of Sky

    Nintendo DS. Oct 12, 2009 in North America. Nov 20, 2009 in Europe.

    The third version of Explorers. It adds more story and more playable characters, five new starters (Phanpy, Riolu, Shinx, Eevee and Vulpix), five Special Episodes and new places such as Shaymin Village and the Spinda Cafe. Its Wonder Mail S does not work with the earlier games.

    Metacritic
    54
    GameRankings
    55.84%

    Famitsu gave 35 out of 40 and added it to its Platinum Hall of Fame. Wikipedia says fans regard it as a cult classic.

  5. Follow-up game

    Gates to Infinity

    Nintendo 3DS. Mar 24, 2013 in North America. May 17, 2013 in Europe.

    A new game with new starters (Pikachu, Oshawott, Tepig, Snivy and Axew), 3D models and no personality quiz. A hub called Pokémon Paradise is new, Magnagates open when you scan round objects with the 3DS, and some dungeons were free or paid DLC.

    Metacritic
    59
    GameRankings
    62.34%

    IGN gave 4.5 and said it fails as both a Pokémon game and a Mystery Dungeon game. GameSpot gave 5, GamesRadar+ 2.5 out of 5 and Destructoid 8.

  6. Follow-up game

    Super Mystery Dungeon

    Nintendo 3DS. Nov 20, 2015 in North America. Feb 19, 2016 in Europe.

    Twenty Pokémon are playable, and you recruit a partner from the other 19. Every Pokémon through Hoopa is in, 720 in all, with 3D graphics and generated dungeons.

    Metacritic
    69

    Nintendo World Report’s review mentions a story of 20 or more hours, and a tutorial of about four hours that it found overlong.

    Destructoid gave 9 and Famitsu 36 out of 40. Critics called it repetitive, and Nintendo Life called it tedious. Nintendo World Report scored it 7.5 out of 10, called the story the weakest in the series and called it the easiest entry point.

  7. Remake

    Rescue Team DX

    Nintendo Switch. Mar 6, 2020 worldwide.

    A remake of the first games, with Red and Blue merged into one. It adds Mega Evolution, autosave and an auto-mode, a painted art style, and 16 Pokémon to choose from after the quiz, drawn from Generations 1 to 3. You switch to the next team member when yours is defeated, and shiny Pokémon appear only as strong foes. Bulbapedia says Western Cave drops from 99 floors to 20 and the team limit rises from 4 to 8.

    Metacritic
    69
    Story
    17.5 hours
    With extras
    32.5 hours
    Everything
    124 hours

    HowLongToBeat, via Deku Deals, says 17½, 32½ and 124. Gfinity cites an older figure, which it does not tie to a version, of about 20 hours for the story and nearly 150 for everything.

    IGN gave 6 and GameSpot 8. Critics found the dungeons repetitive and missed enemy health bars.

What changes across this series

  • Explorers is a sequel with the same format, Generation 4 Pokémon and better Wi-Fi.
  • Explorers of Sky is a third version of Explorers, with more story, more playable characters and new episodes.
  • Gates to Infinity moves to 3D, drops the personality quiz and adds Magnagates.
  • Super Mystery Dungeon lets you recruit a partner from the 19 other playable Pokémon and has 720 Pokémon.
  • Rescue Team DX remakes the first games, with Red and Blue merged into one.
  • Adventure Team, three WiiWare games, came out in Japan only.

What critics said

Metacritic scores run from 54 to 69. Super Mystery Dungeon and Rescue Team DX share the top at 69. Reviewers praised the story and soundtrack of Explorers and criticised repetition across the series.

Short answer: Rescue Team DX is the remake of the first game and is on Switch. Nintendo World Report calls Super Mystery Dungeon the easiest entry point.

    Metacritic
    80

    Famitsu gave 34 out of 40, IGN 9, Nintendo World Report 9, Destructoid 8 and Game Informer 7. IGN, GamesRadar+ and Nintendo Life each count it among the best DS games.

Metacritic 80. IGN, GamesRadar+ and Nintendo Life each count it among the best DS games.

Short answer: A strategy game, not a catching game, and well reviewed.
