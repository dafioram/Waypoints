Zelda, in release orderand what each re-release really changes

Twenty entries from 1986 to 2024, counting the two Oracle games as one. Every port, remaster and remake is filed under the game it came from and marked for how different it is, from the same game on new hardware to a remix with new puzzles. Each entry also has an approximate play time.

The count so far: 33 same-game re-releases, 8 polished versions, 4 remakes and 2 remixes. A rebuilt Ocarina of Time arrives on Switch 2 on Nov 5, 2026.

How different is each version?

  1. Same game 33

    A port, emulation or re-listing of the original. Small fixes at most, nothing you would notice.

    33 version rows across 14 games: the Virtual Console, Switch Online, compilation and handheld re-listings.

  2. Same game, polished 8

    The same content with better visuals, controls or extras: HD remasters, a colour edition, a new mode.

    • A Link to the Past and Four Swords 2002–03
    • Link’s Awakening DX 1998
    • Four Swords Anniversary Edition 2011
    • The Wind Waker HD 2013
    • Twilight Princess HD 2016
    • Skyward Sword HD 2021
    • Breath of the Wild: Nintendo Switch 2 Edition 2025
    • Tears of the Kingdom: Nintendo Switch 2 Edition 2025

  3. Remake 4

    Rebuilt for new hardware with new visuals and controls and some design changes, but the same world and story.

    • Link’s Awakening (2019) 2019
    • Ocarina of Time 3D 2011
    • Ocarina of Time (2026) Nov 5, 2026, not out yet
    • Majora’s Mask 3D 2015

  4. Remix 2

    The same foundation with much of the content rearranged or new, such as revised puzzles or maps.

    • The Legend of Zelda: BS Zelda no Densetsu 1995–97, Japan only
    • Ocarina of Time: Master Quest 2002–03
How the labels work

The four labels are my own judgment, based on the changes each source lists. They are not Nintendo’s terms, and Wikipedia calls some of these “remasters” that I list as remakes. Each version row says what changed so you can disagree. Counts are of the version rows on this page, and one row such as Virtual Console can cover several platforms. Scores and sales figures are as quoted on each game’s Wikipedia page, and sales are for the original release unless stated.

How the play times work

Play times are approximate hours. Where I could, they are HowLongToBeat’s averages, which come from players reporting their own times. I could not open HowLongToBeat’s own pages, so I read those through Deku Deals’ copy of them for Switch games, checked on Oct 14, 2026. For the other games they come from articles: Game Rant says it used HowLongToBeat as a reference, WatchMojo and ProGameGuides do not name a source, and Nintendo Life gives its own estimates. Each card names its source, and the ones without a named source are the roughest.

“Main story” is playing through to the credits, “Main + extras” adds side content, and “Everything” is a full completion, which HowLongToBeat calls Completionist. The articles label their columns a little differently (standard, average, story, completionist), and I have filed them under main story and everything. A missing “Main + extras” means the source did not give one. Averages shift as more players report, and a remake can run shorter than its original, so read these as a sense of scale.

1986Feb · No. 1

The Legend of Zelda

First released: Famicom Disk System, Japan

  • Sold 6.51M

You explore Hyrule from above, one screen at a time, to gather eight fragments of the Triforce of Wisdom and rescue Princess Zelda from Ganon. The map is open and the dungeons are mazes, so where you go next is largely up to you.

  • Seen from overhead on flip screens, with a large outdoor map.
  • Eight dungeons, each guarding a Triforce fragment. Some have secret entrances you find by wandering.
  • Non-linear. Wikipedia notes it is possible to reach the final boss without picking up the sword.
  • A harder Second Quest unlocks when you finish.
  • The US cartridge was one of the first games with a battery inside to save progress, replacing passwords.

Play time approximate hours

  • Main story 8–9 h
  • Everything 10–11 h

Source: Game Rant, 2023

1987Jan · No. 2

Zelda II: The Adventure of Link

First released: Famicom Disk System, Japan

  • Sold 4.38M

Set several years after the first game, Link sets out to wake Princess Zelda from a sleeping spell. A top-down world map is mixed with side-scrolling action, and Link now levels up.

  • Side-scrolling areas sit inside a larger top-down world map.
  • Defeating enemies earns experience points that raise Attack, Magic and Life, each up to seven levels above its start.
  • Eight spells are learned from wise men in eight towns.
  • The only Zelda with lives and 1-up dolls. Wikipedia says the RPG elements have not been used in the canonical entries since.
  • The series’ odd one out, but it introduced the magic meter and Dark Link.

Play time approximate hours

  • Main story 10–11 h
  • Everything 11–12 h

Source: Game Rant, 2023

1991Nov · No. 3

A Link to the Past

First released: Super Nintendo, Japan

  • Sold 4.61M on SNES
  • With GBA 6.5M by 2004

Back to the overhead view of the first game, now on the Super Nintendo. Link sets out to save Hyrule and stop Ganon, in a game that is a distant prequel to the earlier two. Hyrule has a twin, the Dark World, reached through portals.

  • Link can move diagonally, which the first game could not do.
  • Light World and Dark World share a largely matching map but have different enemies, characters and dungeons.
  • Each multi-level dungeon has a large chest holding a key piece of equipment.
  • Sword and shield come early and can be upgraded. Four Heart Pieces make one Heart Container.
  • Wikipedia calls it “among the greatest video games ever made”.

Play time approximate hours

  • Main story 14–15 h
  • Everything 16–17 h

Source: Game Rant, 2023

Versions (4 re-releases)

  1. Original1991–92

    Super Nintendo

    Japan on Nov 21, 1991, North America on Apr 13, 1992, Europe on Sep 24, 1992. Japan’s title was Kamigami no Triforce, “Triforce of the Gods”.

  2. Same game1997

    Satellaview broadcastJapan only

    Super Famicom, Japan only

    An unaltered broadcast of the Japanese original on Mar 2, 1997, without SoundLink support.

  3. Same game, polished2002–03

    A Link to the Past and Four Swords

    Game Boy Advance

    The same adventure with a few touches, packaged with the multiplayer Four Swords (see below).

    What changed
    • Voice clips and sound effects were taken from Ocarina of Time and Majora’s Mask.
    • Finishing Four Swords unlocks the Palace of the Four Sword dungeon in this game.
    • The secret Chris Houlihan room was made inaccessible, though its code remained.
    • Developed by Nintendo and Capcom. North America in 2002, other regions in 2003.
  4. Same game2010s

    Virtual Console and SNES Classic

    Wii, Wii U, New 3DS, mini console

    Emulations of the original, including the Chris Houlihan room. Wikipedia gives no dates for these.

  5. Same game2019

    Nintendo Switch Online

    Nintendo Switch

    Added on Sep 5, 2019 with 19 other SNES games.

Short answer: No remake. The Game Boy Advance version is the only one that differs, and only by sound effects and an extra Four Swords link.

Not a version: A Link Between Worlds (2013) is a sequel set in the same world, not a remake. BS Zelda: Ancient Stone Tablets (1997) is a separate Satellaview follow-up set six years later, not a port.

1993Jun · No. 4

Link’s Awakening

First released: Game Boy, Japan

  • GameRankings 90%
  • Sold 4M

Link is stranded on Koholint Island after a storm wrecks his boat, and must find eight musical instruments to escape. It is one of the few Zelda games not set in Hyrule: there is no Triforce and Zelda gets only a passing mention.

  • A mostly overhead view, but Link can jump, which adds side-scrolling sections.
  • The A and B buttons can each be assigned to a different item.
  • Bosses give heart containers and instruments.
  • Optional extras include a trading sequence, hidden secret seashells, fishing and ocarina songs.
  • Its small-town feel was influenced by Twin Peaks, per Wikipedia.

Play time approximate hours

Original and DX

  • Main story 14–15 h
  • Everything 16–20 h

Source: Game Rant, 2023

2019 remake

  • Main story 14 h
  • Main + extras 16½ h
  • Everything 21 h

Source: HowLongToBeat, via Deku Deals

The remake row is the Switch game. Game Rant gives one line for every version of the game, which I have used for the older ones. The two come out about the same.

Versions (3 re-releases)

  1. Original1993

    Game Boy

    Japan on Jun 6, 1993, North America on Aug 6, 1993, Europe on Dec 1, 1993.

  2. Same game, polished1998

    Link’s Awakening DX

    Game Boy Color

    Full colour, still playable on the original Game Boy. Adds an optional colour-based dungeon and a camera shop with 12 photos that print on the Game Boy Printer.

    What changed
    • The extra colour dungeon cannot be played on non-colour models.
    • Released on Nintendo 3DS Virtual Console in June 2011 and on Switch Online on Feb 8, 2023. Both are the DX version.
  3. Remake2019

    Link’s Awakening (2019)

    Nintendo Switch

    A rebuild by Grezzo with new toy-like 3D visuals, released Sep 20, 2019. Engadget’s review says the gameplay and level design are “almost entirely unchanged”.

    What changed
    • Look: “toy-ish 3D characters in a pseudo-3D world” with a tilt-shift effect and an orchestral soundtrack (Engadget).
    • Controls were updated a little for Switch: two action items on X and Y, the shield on ZL (Engadget).
    • The Game Boy Color’s extra dungeon is included (Engadget).
    • Adds an optional Chamber Dungeons feature that lets you build dungeons for Dampé. Engadget’s reviewer found it “nowhere near as fun or open-ended” as the main game.
  4. Same game2021

    Game & Watch: The Legend of Zelda

    Handheld

    Includes the original Game Boy version, alongside the first two Zelda games.

Short answer: The 2019 version looks completely different but plays like the same game. If you want the old version, DX is the one on Switch Online.

Not a version: Echoes of Wisdom (2024) borrows the 2019 remake’s look and began as an expansion of its dungeon editor. It is a new game, covered below.

1998Nov · No. 5

Ocarina of Time

First released: Nintendo 64, Japan

  • Metacritic 99
  • Sold 7M+

The first 3D Zelda. Link plays through Hyrule on a quest to stop the evil king Ganondorf, and the quest involves travelling through time.

  • Z-targeting: lock onto an enemy and Link keeps facing it while you move, and projectiles aim automatically.
  • Context-sensitive buttons let one button do several jobs, which simplified the controls.
  • Ocarina songs solve music puzzles and teleport you to places you have visited.
  • Link can switch between younger and adult forms, each with different abilities.

Play time approximate hours

  • Main story 25–27 h
  • Everything 37–39 h

Source: Game Rant, 2023, WatchMojo

Game Rant lists the 3D remake at these same figures, and WatchMojo also says the two are about the same length.

Versions (6 re-releases)

  1. Original1998

    Nintendo 64

    Japan on Nov 21, North America on Nov 23, Europe on Dec 11 and Australia on Dec 18, 1998.

  2. Remix2002–03

    Master Quest

    GameCube

    A harder alternate version with revised puzzles, first released officially as a bonus disc for Wind Waker preorders in Japan and North America in 2002.

    What changed
    • Japan on Nov 28, 2002, North America on Feb 18, 2003, Europe on May 3, 2003. In Europe it came in bundles.
    • Ura Zelda, the 64DD version, was never released on N64. It was later ported to GameCube as Master Quest (Wikipedia).
    • The 3DS remake includes it as an unlock. Nintendo Life describes that version as “the same adventure” with a mirrored world, tougher enemies and more complex dungeon puzzles.
  3. Same game2003

    Collector’s Edition

    GameCube

    The standard game run as an emulation at 640×480 with progressive scan. Targeting moves to the L button.

    What changed
    • This disc does not contain Master Quest.
  4. Same game2007, 2015

    Virtual Console

    Wii, Wii U

    The Wii version in 2007 lacked controller vibration, so the Stone of Agony item did nothing. The Wii U version on Jul 2, 2015 restored it.

  5. Remake2011

    Ocarina of Time 3D

    Nintendo 3DS

    New visuals and controls by Nintendo and Grezzo, plus extra modes. Wikipedia calls it a “remaster”. I list it as a remake because it also changes controls, adds modes and eases a dungeon.

    What changed
    • Touch screen and gyroscope controls. Nintendo Life says the touch screen gives quick access to items, the ocarina, the map and Navi.
    • A Boss Challenge mode and hint videos for players who are stuck.
    • A Water Temple with reduced difficulty (Wikipedia).
    • Master Quest unlocks after you finish the main game.
  6. Same game2021

    Nintendo Switch Online

    Nintendo 64 library

    Added on Oct 25, 2021 to the N64 library, which needs the Expansion Pack tier.

  7. RemakeNov 5, 2026

    Ocarina of Time (2026)Not out yet

    Nintendo Switch 2

    A rebuilt version that Nintendo says was “remade with completely overhauled visuals, fully voiced cutscenes, expanded character dialogue”. The developer has not been named in the coverage I read.

    What changed
    • Digital price $59.99, physical $69.99 (Gematsu). Switch 2 only.
    • Visuals redone “down to the textures of each material”, with faster load times and an orchestral soundtrack (Nintendo, as quoted by Gematsu).
    • New button commands for jumping and running, camera control on the right stick, and motion aiming (Gematsu and Game Informer).
    • A Threads of Time feature shows your story progress and next objective. You can hum a learned song into the microphone to play it.
    • Series producer Eiji Aonuma calls it “a faithful interpretation using modern technologies”, and the trailers keep the world layout and lock-on combat (Zelda Universe).
    • Not stated in any coverage I read: the developer, and whether Master Quest is included.

Short answer: One rebuild is out (the 3DS one, 2011) and a second arrives on Nov 5, 2026. Master Quest is the version with different puzzles, and the 3DS remake includes it.

2000Apr · No. 6

Majora’s Mask

First released: Nintendo 64, Japan

  • Metacritic 95
  • Sold 3.36M on N64

Link arrives in Termina, a parallel world that Wikipedia calls a darker, more unsettling version of Hyrule, where a moon will crash into the land in three days. It is the sequel to Ocarina of Time.

  • A repeating three-day cycle of about 54 minutes of real time. The Song of Time resets you to the first day, and you keep your items, masks and songs.
  • 24 masks. Three transform Link into a Deku Scrub, a Goron or a Zora, each with its own abilities.
  • Built on an upgraded Ocarina of Time engine and reusing its assets and character models.
  • The Nintendo 64 version requires the Expansion Pak add-on.

Play time approximate hours

  • Main story 20–23 h
  • Everything 31–35 h

Source: WatchMojo, ProGameGuides

Estimates from WatchMojo and ProGameGuides. Neither names its source, so this is a rougher guide than most. WatchMojo says the 3D remake can run up to 38 hours for everything.

Versions (4 re-releases)

  1. Original2000

    Nintendo 64

    Japan on Apr 27, North America on Oct 26 and Europe on Nov 17, 2000.

  2. Same game2003–04

    Collector’s Edition

    GameCube

    An emulation with minor button-icon changes and slightly higher native resolution with progressive scan. GameSpot’s Ricardo Torres found the frame rate “appears choppier”.

  3. Same game2009, 2016

    Virtual Console

    Wii, Wii U

    The Wii version in 2009 and the Wii U version in 2016.

  4. Remake2015

    Majora’s Mask 3D

    Nintendo 3DS

    Rebuilt by Grezzo with improved character models, stereoscopic 3D, altered boss fights and an added fishing minigame. The team also reviewed moments it found unreasonable, a list it called “what in the world”.

    What changed
    • Supports the New 3DS’s second analog stick (Wikipedia).
    • Zelda Universe’s review says the Bombers’ Notebook was heavily overhauled into a “quest log”, owl statues now work as real save points, and the Song of Double Time lets you pick the hour to skip to.
    • The same review says Odolwa and Gyorg are “much improved”, Goht is weaker than the original, and Zora swimming is slower and more precise by default.
    • The three-day limit still exists but is, in that reviewer’s words, “much easier to manage”.
  5. Same game2022

    Nintendo Switch Online

    Nintendo 64 library

    Added on Feb 25, 2022 to the N64 library, which needs the Expansion Pack tier of the service.

Short answer: The 3D version is a real rework of how the game manages time and saves. The other versions are the same game.

Not a version: Majora’s Mask is a new game built from Ocarina of Time’s engine and assets, not a version of it.

2001Feb · No. 7

Oracle of Seasons and Oracle of Ages

First released: Game Boy Color, Japan

  • Sold 3.96M each

Two Game Boy Color games released together. Each is complete on its own, but they link: finishing either gives a password for an alternate version of the other, and items, upgrades and rings carry across.

  • Oracle of Seasons is set in Holodrum. Its Rod of Seasons changes the season, and Wikipedia describes the game as focused on action.
  • Oracle of Ages is set in Labrynna. Its Harp of Ages lets Link travel through time, and Wikipedia describes the game as concentrating on puzzles.
  • Both use an overhead view.
  • Developed by Flagship, then Capcom’s scenario-writing subsidiary, and published by Nintendo.
  • Critics faulted inconsistent sound quality, per Wikipedia.

2002Dec · No. 8

Four Swords

First released: Game Boy Advance, North America

  • Metacritic 95

A multiplayer-first Zelda for two to four players, each with their own Game Boy Advance and copy of the game. Players cooperate through randomized, puzzle-filled dungeons while competing for rupees. It is set before Ocarina of Time.

  • Each dungeon has three levels, and the third is a boss fight.
  • Puzzles scale to the number of connected players.
  • Each player holds one item at a time, swapped at pedestals.
  • The rupee leader at the end of a level wins a special prize, but rupees are shared in the long run.
  • Cubed3’s review called the lack of single-player “a let-down”.

Play time approximate hours

Anniversary Edition

  • Main story 2–3 h

Source: Nintendo Life

Nintendo Life’s 2011 review of the Anniversary Edition says it takes “around two-three hours” to work through the four main stages, and the reviewer’s own multiplayer run took about three. I found no dependable figure for finishing everything.

Versions (2 re-releases)

  1. Original2002–03

    Game Boy Advance

    A mode of A Link to the Past & Four Swords. North America on Dec 3, 2002, Japan on Mar 14, 2003 and Europe on Mar 28, 2003. Wikipedia says its 95 is the highest Metacritic score for a Game Boy Advance game.

  2. Same game, polished2011

    Four Swords Anniversary Edition

    Nintendo DSi, Nintendo 3DS

    A Grezzo port that adds a single-player mode where you control two Links, plus two new areas, Realm of Memories and a harder Hero’s Trial.

    What changed
    • Released Sep 28, 2011 and free to DSi and 3DS owners until Feb 20, 2012.
    • Offered free again from Jan 31 to Feb 2, 2014 to promote A Link Between Worlds.
  3. Same game2024

    Nintendo Switch Online

    Game Boy Advance library

    Added on Jun 18, 2024 as A Link to the Past & Four Swords, an Expansion Pack title.

Short answer: The Anniversary Edition adds a single-player mode, which the original did not have.

The sources I read do not say what camera view Four Swords uses, so I have not described it.

2002Dec · No. 9

The Wind Waker

First released: GameCube, Japan

  • Metacritic 96
  • Sold 4.6M

Link sails the Great Sea, a world of islands, in a cartoon look made with cel shading. Wikipedia calls the setting “a departure for the series”. He explores islands and dungeons to gain the power to face Ganon.

  • Sailing on the King of Red Lions. The wind blows in one of eight directions and a tailwind gives top speed.
  • The Wind Waker is a baton you conduct with. The first tune changes the wind, and others warp you or turn night into day.
  • The Great Sea has 49 gridded sections, and a sea chart tracks islands.
  • The cel-shaded look was revealed at Space World 2001 and fans pushed back, some mocking it as “Celda”.

2004Nov · No. 11

The Minish Cap

First released: Game Boy Advance, Japan

  • Metacritic 89
  • Sold 1.76M

Link meets Ezlo, a magical talking cap, during the Picori Festival. Ezlo lets him shrink to the size of the tiny Minish. The art is cartoonish, like The Wind Waker, and the game keeps the top-down elements of A Link to the Past.

  • Shrinking works through portals and changes what Link can reach. A puddle he can walk over is too deep at Minish size.
  • Three new items: the Mole Mitts, the Gust Jar and the Cane of Pacci.
  • Kinstones are collectibles split into two halves. Matching pieces open new things or award prizes.
  • In certain areas Link can make copies of himself. It is not co-op: the game is single-player and borrows the idea from the Four Swords games.
  • Developed by Capcom and Flagship. Wikipedia calls it a prequel to Four Swords.

Twilight Princess

First released: Wii and GameCube

  • Metacritic 96 GC, 95 Wii
  • Sold 8.85M by 2015

A third-person Zelda, darker in tone, in which Hyrule is being engulfed by a corrupted parallel dimension, the Twilight Realm. Link is helped by Midna, a mysterious imp. It was a launch game for the Wii and the last first-party game for the GameCube.

  • Nine dungeons and a large overworld, travelled on foot, on horseback with Epona, or by Midna’s warping.
  • Link can become a wolf, which lets him dig, track scents and bite, but not use his sword or secondary items.
  • Horseback combat was a central goal, following advice from Shigeru Miyamoto.
  • The twilight areas “were intended to make players feel uncomfortable”, per Wikipedia.
  • Two launch versions: the Wii version has motion controls, a mirrored world map and four equippable secondary items. The GameCube version keeps the original map orientation, has a free camera and two items.

2007Jun · No. 13

Phantom Hourglass

First released: Nintendo DS, Japan

  • Metacritic 90
  • Sold 4.13M by Mar 2008

The direct sequel to The Wind Waker, on the DS, with 3D cel-shaded graphics and an overhead camera. Link works to save his friend Tetra from the antagonist Bellum, with help from Captain Linebeck and his ship.

  • The stylus is central: you move Link by pointing, tap to attack or interact, draw shapes to open doors and plot ship courses on a sea chart. The microphone is used too.
  • Two modes of play: sailing between islands, and exploring islands and dungeons on foot.
  • The Temple of the Ocean King drains Link’s life with a curse. The Phantom Hourglass gives limited safe time, invincible Phantoms patrol, and the temple resets each time you leave.
  • A one-on-one battle mode supports local play, Wi-Fi and Download Play.

2011Nov · No. 15

Skyward Sword

First released: Wii

  • Metacritic 93
  • Sold 3.67M Wii, 4.15M HD

Link lives on Skyloft, a floating town in the sky, and the story moves between the sky and the surface below. Wikipedia calls it the first game in the series timeline and says it tells the origin of the Master Sword.

  • Needs Wii MotionPlus. Sword moves follow the direction you swing the Wii Remote.
  • A stamina meter limits sprinting, climbing and carrying heavy objects.
  • Shields (except the Hylian Shield) take damage from poorly timed parries and can break for good.
  • Link flies between islands on a giant bird called a Loftwing.
  • Items and equipment are upgraded with materials gathered from enemies, the world and chests.

2013Nov · No. 16

A Link Between Worlds

First released: Nintendo 3DS

  • Metacritic 91
  • Sold 4.26M by 2022

Nintendo describes it as “a successor to A Link to the Past, set within the same world but featuring new mechanics, new dungeons, and an original story”. Link must rescue the Seven Sages and stop a villain named Yuga. Wikipedia says it is mostly top-down, with stereoscopic 3D graphics.

  • Link can merge into walls as a flat mural and move along them. The energy gauge drains while he does.
  • Items are not found in dungeons. They are rented or bought from the merchant Ravio, and rented items return to Ravio if Link dies.
  • Rented items share one energy gauge instead of using ammunition.
  • Many of the dungeons can be tackled in any order.
  • The world is based on A Link to the Past’s, with many areas reworked, and a second kingdom, Lorule, mirrors Hyrule’s layout.

2015Oct · No. 17

Tri Force Heroes

First released: Nintendo 3DS

  • Metacritic 73
  • Sold 1.36M by 2020

Three Links are summoned by Hytopia, a fashion-focused kingdom, to deal with a curse on its princess. It is built for three players, locally or online, and a single player uses doll-like companions called Doppels.

  • Players take items at the start of each level and work together to solve puzzles and fight enemies.
  • Links can stack into a totem, so the top player reaches higher places, attacks or is thrown across gaps.
  • Collected items craft outfits that grant hidden abilities. The team shares one heart meter.
  • Players communicate with icons on the touch screen.
  • Its style is “strongly based on” A Link Between Worlds, mixed with The Wind Waker’s look (Wikipedia and Zelda Wiki).

2017Mar · No. 18

Breath of the Wild

First released: Wii U and Nintendo Switch

  • Metacritic 97 Switch
  • Sold 34.51M by Mar 2025

An open-world Zelda. Link, an amnesiac warrior, wakes on the Great Plateau and is free to explore Hyrule, with the goal of saving Zelda and stopping Calamity Ganon. The story can be completed in nonlinear order.

  • Link can climb, swim and glide with a paraglider, limited by stamina.
  • A physics engine and a “chemistry engine” govern how things behave. In storms, for example, metal attracts lightning.
  • Weapons and shields break with use. The Master Sword is the only unbreakable weapon, but it needs recharging.
  • Cooking turns food and materials into meals and elixirs. Shrines offer puzzles or robot battles, and Divine Beasts act as extended puzzles.
  • A paid Expansion Pass added two DLC packs in 2017: The Master Trials and The Champions’ Ballad.

Play time approximate hours

  • Main story 50½ h
  • Main + extras 98½ h
  • Everything 193½ h

Source: HowLongToBeat, via Deku Deals

First released: Nintendo Switch

  • Metacritic 96 Switch

A sequel to Breath of the Wild, set years later. It keeps that game’s open world and Hyrule map and adds floating sky islands and a large underground area called the Depths. Link searches for Princess Zelda and works to stop Ganondorf from destroying Hyrule.

  • Five new powers replace the Sheikah Slate’s abilities: Ultrahand, Fuse, Ascend, Recall and Autobuild.
  • Zonai devices let you build vehicles and tools.
  • Sky islands hold puzzles, treasure, enemies and bosses.
  • The Depths need light sources and contain hazards like lava and gloom.
  • Shrines and Korok seeds are scattered across the world.

Looks similar, but it is a new game

Sequels that reuse an older game’s world, engine or style. These are not versions of the games they resemble.

Majora’s Mask
Majora’s Mask is built on an upgraded Ocarina of Time engine and reuses its assets and character models. It is set in a new world with a new structure, so it is a sequel, not a version.
A Link Between Worlds
A Link Between Worlds is set in the same world as A Link to the Past, with many areas reworked and a second kingdom, Lorule, that mirrors Hyrule’s layout. Its dungeons, mechanics and story are new.
Four Swords Adventures
Four Swords Adventures is similar to Four Swords in gameplay and presentation, according to Wikipedia, but with fixed maps and a more continuous story.
The Minish Cap
The Minish Cap borrows the idea of making copies of Link from the Four Swords games but is a single-player game.
Phantom Hourglass
Phantom Hourglass is the direct sequel to The Wind Waker and keeps its cel-shaded look, but it is built for the DS and its touch screen.
Spirit Tracks
Spirit Tracks is set a century after Phantom Hourglass and continues its stylus-driven style.
Tri Force Heroes
Tri Force Heroes is “strongly based on” the look of A Link Between Worlds, per Zelda Wiki, and also mixes in The Wind Waker’s style.
Tears of the Kingdom
Tears of the Kingdom keeps Breath of the Wild’s Hyrule map and adds floating sky islands and a large underground area, the Depths, with new powers.
Echoes of Wisdom
Echoes of Wisdom shares the 2019 Link’s Awakening remake’s look and began as an expansion of that game’s dungeon editor before becoming an original game.

Not covered

You asked for the main games only. Left out: Hyrule Warriors and Age of Calamity, Cadence of Hyrule, the Philips CD-i games, Link’s Crossbow Training, the Tingle games, and mini-games. The Game & Watch handheld appears only where it carries a main game. BS Zelda no Densetsu appears only as a version of the first game, and BS Zelda: Ancient Stone Tablets is not covered. As you asked, Oracle of Seasons and Ages, Four Swords, Four Swords Adventures, Tri Force Heroes and Echoes of Wisdom all count as main-line here.
