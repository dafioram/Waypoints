Dragon Ball games

75 console, handheld and PC games from 1986 to 2025, sorted by what kind of game they are. Just under half are fighters.

Covers the Dragon Ball Z era and the games around it, including Japan-only releases. Mobile, arcade, crossovers and games that only wear a Dragon Ball theme are left out; see what is not covered.

Quick answers

Are most of them one-on-one fighters?
Close to half. 35 of 75 (47%) are fighting games: 14 in 2D and 21 in 3D. The rest are 20 RPGs, 6 card games, 9 action and platform games, 3 online games and 2 interactive-story discs. Not every fighter is a duel: Battle of Z, Tenkaichi Tag Team and Sparking! Zero are team or tag fights, and I did not check every game's mode list.
Which ones are on Steam, and where else do they run?
8: Xenoverse, Xenoverse 2, FighterZ, Kakarot, Sparking! Zero, The Breakers, World Mission and Gekishin Squadra. All eight also came out on consoles. The Steam table shows the year each one reached each platform.
Which ones never left Japan?
21 are marked Japan only: 17 from 1986 to 1995 (Famicom, Super Famicom, PC Engine, Saturn, Game Boy and Playdia), plus Kai: Ultimate Butōden on DS and the three Heroes games on 3DS. That means my sources list no other region, not that I checked every store.
What is the oldest game, and the newest?
The oldest is Dragon Daihikyō, a shoot 'em up from September 1986. The newest release is Gekishin Squadra (September 2025); Sparking! Zero reached Switch and Switch 2 in November 2025. Xenoverse 3 is announced for 2027.

Beyond the fighters

A few of the games that are not duels. Each name links to its entry in the genre lists.

  • A shoot 'em up from 1986

    Dragon Daihikyō

    The earliest game I found is an overhead shoot 'em up for the Super Cassette Vision, released in Japan on Sep 27 1986. It is neither a fighter nor an RPG.

  • Trading cards you scan

    Dragon Ball Z: Gekitō Tenkaichi Budōkai

    A Famicom fighter that needs the Datach barcode reader. The cards you scan set your fighter's stats. Japan only, Dec 29 1992.

  • Choose your own adventure

    Dragon Ball Z: Shin Saiyajin Zenmetsu Keikaku, Chikyū-Hen, Dragon Ball Z Gaiden: Shin Saiyajin Zenmetsu Keikaku, Uchū-Hen

    Two 1994 Bandai Playdia discs, both Japan only, described as choose-your-own-adventure games with animated scenes. I could not find more about how they play.

  • RPGs with real-time duels

    Dragon Ball Z: Super Gokuden, Totsugeki-Hen, Dragon Ball Z: Super Gokuden, Kakusei-Hen

    Super Famicom action RPGs with a text-driven story, branching choices, mini-games and real-time one-on-one fights. Japan only, 1995.

  • Seven survivors, one raider

    Dragon Ball: The Breakers

    An eight-player survival game from 2022. Survivors try to escape in a time machine by using keys scattered around the map, or fight back by briefly transforming into Dragon Ball heroes. The raider is a series villain who grows stronger during the match.

  • Card battles on 3DS and Switch

    Dragon Ball Heroes: Ultimate Mission, Dragon Ball Heroes: Ultimate Mission 2, Dragon Ball Heroes: Ultimate Mission X, Super Dragon Ball Heroes: World Mission

    The three Heroes: Ultimate Mission games on 3DS are Japan only and based on an arcade trading-card game. World Mission (2019) is a card battle game on Switch and Steam, set in a world where a Dragon Ball card game is popular.

  • Fighting with your whole body

    Dragon Ball Z for Kinect

    Dragon Ball Z for Kinect (Xbox 360, October 2012) is a motion-controlled action game.

  • An MMORPG that no longer exists

    Dragon Ball Online

    Dragon Ball Online ran on Windows from 2010. The servers shut down in October 2013.

My pick to start with: Dragon Ball FighterZ

For the 16-bit originals, try Super Butōden 2 on Super Famicom (Japan and Europe).

A turn-based DS RPG from Monolith Soft that got an English release in 2009, so you do not need to import it.

Announced, not out yet

Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 is announced for 2027 on Windows (Steam), PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. As of Nintendo Life's July 2026 report, no Nintendo platform is confirmed. It is the dashed dot on the chart. It was previously teased as “Age 1000”.

Xenoverse 2's last DLC, Future Saga Chapter 4, was scheduled for July 8 2026.

What is not covered

  • Mobile games such as Dokkan Battle, Legends, Bucchigiri Match and the mobile version of Gekishin Squadra.
  • Arcade-only games. Super Dragon Ball Z is here only because of its PlayStation 2 release.
  • Crossovers where Dragon Ball is one franchise among several: Jump Force, J-Stars Victory VS and Jump Ultimate Stars.
  • Toys and collectibles that are not video games, such as the Terebikko toy and the Collectible CD Picture Cards.
  • Games that only wear a Dragon Ball theme, such as puzzle or Tetris-style software. You asked me to skip these.
  • Separate listings for re-releases. A few are mentioned in a game's note, such as the Budokai HD Collection.

What I could not confirm

  • The count. 75 is the number of games I could verify, not a claim that no others exist. Obscure Japan-only releases are the likeliest gaps.
  • Genres I inferred or found only in one place: Dragon Ball Z III: Ressen Jinzōningen (inferred from its neighbors), Idainaru Son Gokū Densetsu on PC Engine (seven Goku battles with cutscenes, but no source I read calls it a fighter), and the two Playdia discs (one source calls them choose-your-own-adventure games). Each carries a “Genre not fully confirmed” flag.
  • How battles work in the two Game Boy RPGs, Gokū Hishōden and Gokū Gekitōden. My sources disagree, so I did not say.
  • The genre of the Heroes: Ultimate Mission games. They are based on an arcade card game, and Siliconera called Ultimate Mission X a card battle RPG. I did not verify the first two individually.
  • Whether the Switch version of Kakarot (Sep 24 2021) runs natively or streams from the cloud. The sources I read do not say.
  • Whether Gekishin Squadra is on Switch 2. The source I read lists PlayStation, Switch, Steam, iOS and Android, and does not mention Switch 2.
  • Whether the last Xenoverse 2 DLC, Future Saga Chapter 4, shipped on July 8 2026 as scheduled.
  • Dates. Each row shows the first release I found in the regions I found it. Where only one region appears, I did not look up the others. Budokai 2 and Legendary Super Warriors show only a year, and Kai: Ultimate Butōden has two dates in my sources (Feb 3 and Feb 4 2011).
  • Japan-only flags. A game is flagged when the sources I read list no release outside Japan. I did not check every regional store.
